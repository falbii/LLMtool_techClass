- Products: Fischer−Tropsch fuels, methanol, methane, dimethyl ether, ammonia, urea, olefins, aromatics.
- Locations: Iceland, The Netherlands, Spain, Chile; final location: Basel, Switzerland.
- CO₂ feedstock: low-temperature solid adsorption direct air capture (LT DAC), high-temperature aqueous absorption DAC (HT DAC), point-source flue-gas capture, including cement production sites.
- Electrolysis: alkaline water electrolysis (AEC), proton exchange membrane electrolysis (PEM), solid oxide electrolysis (SOEC).
- Near future: by 2035. Long-term future: post-2035 but by 2050.
- Reference unit: one metric ton of final product; Fischer−Tropsch (FT) fuels, olefins, and aromatics use one metric ton of the mix.
- Investment lifetime: 25 years.
- Plant operation: 8000 h per year; first year: 30%; second year: 70% of total capacity.
- Investment allocation: 30% at two years before first year of production; 50% at one year before production; 20% in first year of production.
- Debt to equity ratio: 1.5.

- Annual CO₂ input: 360 kt CO₂/y.
- Climeworks’ Mammoth DAC plant nominal CO₂ capture capacity: 36 kt per year under full operation; multimegaton scale by 2030.
- Monetary values: 2020 euros.
- CAPEX scaling factor: 0.7; no scaling for DAC and H₂ electrolysis.
- Baseline mass and energy balances: CO₂ from LT DAC; AEC.
- Haber−Bosch process energy balance includes electricity for the air separation unit obtaining N₂ from air.
- MTO process (methanol-to-olefins): thermally highly integrated process.
- Mixed product output: FT fuels, olefins, aromatics.
- Production pathways: FT fuels based on reverse water gas shift (RWGS); FT fuels based on electrochemical reduction (ER) of CO₂; methanol (MeOH); methane (CH₄); dimethyl ether (DME); ammonia (NH₃); urea; olefins; aromatics via methanol-to-aromatics (MTA).

- Combustion emissions from CO₂-based fuels are carbon neutral until 2040 for CO₂ from challenging-to-decarbonize sectors.
- Infrastructure construction and decommissioning GHG emissions are neglected.
- Fossil fuel comparators: 94 kg CO₂ eq/GJ LHV; 80 kg CO₂ eq/GJ LHV.
- EU fossil polyethylene terephthalate (PET) end-of-life: 60% recycling, 20% incineration, 20% landfilling.
- ecoinvent 3.9.1 cutoff database datasets: `"ammonia, anhydrous, liquid RER without RU ammonia production, steam reforming, liquid"`; `"urea RER market for urea"`.
- Locations: Spain, The Netherlands, Iceland, Chile.
- Iceland: geothermal electricity and low-temperature (LT) heat (<100 °C); colocated at Climeworks’ DAC plant in Hellisheidi.
- The Netherlands: offshore wind near the port of Rotterdam; LT residual heat from industrial processes.
- Spain and Chile: photovoltaic (PV) electricity; LT heat from industrial processes or solar thermal collectors.
- Tabernas and Sierra Gorda: high direct normal irradiance.

- Long-term future production costs: Chile on the order of 5% lower than Spain, 15% lower than Iceland, and almost 45% lower than The Netherlands.
- Under average (baseline) assumptions for Chile, MSPs of all products except methane are less than 2.5 times the prices of their fossil counterparts.
- FT fuels: European Refining Association average long-term future production cost below 1.9 €/l (2200 €/t) in Chile and below 2.0 €/l (2350 €/t) in South Europe; baseline discount rate 6% versus 8%; cost reduction 20−30% with a discount rate of 4%.
- SOEC can reduce MSP by 42−218 €/t in The Netherlands; cost benefits in Chile are negligible: 3−11 €/t_product.
- Life cycle GHG emissions versus fossil-based counterparts: FT fuels (RWGS) 55−88% lower; FT fuels (ER) 54−92% lower; methanol 65−91% lower; methane 62−94% lower; DME 77−96%; ammonia 72−96%; urea 68−89%; olefins 50−87%; aromatics 55−87%.

- Products from The Netherlands have around 6%, 50%, and 55% lower life cycle GHG emissions than those imported from Iceland, Spain, and Chile, respectively.
- DME production in Chile with AEC: 3.6 times higher life cycle GHG emissions than in The Netherlands.
- Methane production with SOEC: assuming 90% methane boiler efficiency, production capacity of 124.6 kt CH_d4/y, and lower heating value of 49.9 MJ/kg CH_d4, around a third (41.6 kt CH_d4) of produced CCU methane needs to be reused to reduce life cycle GHG emissions of the SOEC operation.
- Near future: averaging all products and electrolyzers, production in Iceland is 45%, 29%, and 17% cheaper than in The Netherlands, Spain, and Chile, respectively.
- Cost per ton of CO_2 avoided [€/t CO_d2 eq,av]: near (by 2035) and long-term future (post-2035); AEC, PEM, SOEC; CO_2 from LT DAC.

- Reducing operating hours from 8000 to 4000 can be compensated by cheaper electricity.
- Central European countries: long-term future electricity prices below 25−50 €/MWh during most hours between March and October.
- Sensitivity analysis: discount rate 5 to 8% (6% baseline); scaling rate 0.6 to 0.9 (0.7 baseline); operating hours 8000 h to 4000 h. No scaling factor for DAC, electrolysis, and cooling units.
